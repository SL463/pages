Sleeper Draft Review · MPMS 2026

The Picks That Made the Room Go Quiet

A round-by-round read of the MPMS 2026 draft board, flagging the five selections that broke furthest from how this class was actually valued heading into the season — plus five more that deserve an asterisk.

  • 10 TEAMS
  • HALF-PPR
  • SNAKE · 15 RDS
  • 1.5 MIN / PICK
  • DRAFTED SEP 3, 2026

Ten managers, fifteen rounds, one clock running down every ninety seconds. Most of this board played out the way boring, correct drafts do — early running backs, receivers stacked through the middle rounds, kickers and defenses mopped up at the very end. But five picks broke from the script hard enough to make the rest of the league sit up. Ranked below from surprising to stunning, with the case for each pull straight from the board.

Top 5 Surprising Moves

01

panksa takes Gibbs 1.1 — and lets Ja'Marr Chase fall to 1.3

RB · DET Round 1 · Pick 1.1 · Overall No. 1 panksa

Ja'Marr Chase entered the season as the closest thing this format has to a consensus 1.1 — a true bell-cow receiver with no committee to split touches with. panksa passed on him anyway and started the draft with Jahmyr Gibbs, and Chase didn't even go second: Bijan Robinson (1.2) and Christian McCaffrey (1.4) both came off the board first, dropping Chase all the way to 1.3. Betting the entire draft's opening statement on a receiving back over the presumptive best player in the format is the single boldest claim on the whole board.

02

The quarterback room splits in two

QB Picks 2.8 & 3.4 vs. 6.4, 6.10 & 9.6

paulkimmm broke first, taking Josh Allen at 2.8 (No. 18 overall), and sophiamhwang followed one round later with Jordan Love at 3.4 (No. 24) — both gone before a single tight end or third-string running back left the board. Then the position vanished. Joe Burrow didn't go until 6.4 (No. 54), Jalen Hurts fell all the way to 6.10 (No. 60), and Justin Herbert — a top-five arm most seasons — wasn't off the board until 9.6 (No. 86), a full 62 picks after Allen. Same twelve-team room, same scoring format, and two wildly different theories of when a quarterback is worth the pick.

03

Puka Nacua goes 1.5, ahead of three first-round backs

WR · LAR Round 1 · Pick 1.5 · Overall No. 5 marylivengood

Taking the second receiver off the board isn't unusual — taking him ahead of Jonathan Taylor, James Cook, and Saquon Barkley is. marylivengood's 1.5 selection of Puka Nacua bet a proven, three-down workhorse-back class would keep falling, and it did: all three of those backs went in the next five picks. It's a defensible read on a receiver who commanded a massive target share in 2025, but it's also the pick that forced three teams behind her to scramble for their RB1 a round earlier than they planned.

04

Saquon Barkley closes out Round 1

RB · PHI Round 1 · Pick 1.10 · Overall No. 10 mayavenkat11

Barkley was the league's rushing leader as recently as last season, yet he was the sixth and final running back to come off the board in Round 1 — behind Gibbs, Robinson, McCaffrey, Taylor, and Cook. Landing at the very last pick of the round, rather than anywhere in the 1.1–1.4 range his 2024 workload would suggest, reads as the room pricing in real mileage and age concerns on an aging feature back, even one who just posted an MVP-caliber year.

05

Streaming positions jump the line

K · DAL DST · HOU Picks 10.4, 11.7 & 12.3

Kickers and defenses are supposed to be the last thing a manager thinks about — the punt positions everyone streams off the waiver wire in November. Not here. javycane grabbed the Texans' defense at 10.4 (No. 94), Brandon Aubrey went to javycane again at 11.7 (No. 107), and CRUNCHTIME1 took Denver's defense at 12.3 (No. 113) — all three three-to-five rounds ahead of where the rest of the league's roster construction suggests these positions "should" go, with plenty of skill players still on the board.

Honorable Mentions

The tight end free-fall

3.1 → 8.6

Brock Bowers went 3.1 (No. 21) as the clear TE1 — then the position cratered. George Kittle, a top-tier option most years, didn't come off the board until 8.6 (No. 76), a 55-pick gap between the top two tight ends on the entire board.

DK Metcalf's Pittsburgh discount

Pick 9.4 · No. 84

A receiver of Metcalf's pedigree lasting until Round 9 suggests the room is still pricing in real uncertainty around his fit after the offseason trade to Pittsburgh's offense.

Jayden Daniels waits through seven rounds

Pick 8.7 · No. 77

Coming off one of the more electrifying rookie seasons at the position, Daniels going nearly 60 picks after Josh Allen shows just how much the early QB run at 2.8 and 3.4 was about specific names, not the position as a whole.

The Round 6 quarterback pile-up

Picks 6.3, 6.4 & 6.10

Drake Maye (6.3), Joe Burrow (6.4), and Jalen Hurts (6.10) all left the board within a seven-pick window — three teams independently deciding the same late-round moment was finally the right time to fill the position.

McCaffrey still goes top-4

Pick 1.4 · No. 4

Two injury-shortened seasons in the last three didn't stop sophiamhwang from spending a top-four pick on McCaffrey — a vote of confidence the rest of the room clearly didn't share for Barkley six picks later.
